# Product Requirements Document (PRD)
## Departmental Store ERP, Smart POS & Business Management System

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Initial implementation specification  
**Date:** 26 October 2026  
**Product type:** Custom retail management platform  
**Initial deployment:** One departmental store, 2–3 billing counters  
**Primary market:** India  
**Primary currency:** INR (₹)  
**Tax framework:** Indian GST, subject to applicable rules and professional validation

---

# 1. Executive Summary

## 1.1 Product vision

Build a complete departmental store business management system that integrates billing, inventory, purchasing, supplier management, accounting, GST workflows, barcode printing, voice commands, employee management and remote business monitoring.

The system will be tailored to the operational requirements of a physical departmental store. It will provide a fast Windows-based billing application for cashiers, a central ERP backend for business transactions, offline capabilities for network interruptions, and a cloud-connected mobile experience for the owner.

The product must be reliable enough for everyday retail operations and extensible enough to support additional departments, devices and branches in the future.

## 1.2 Problem statement

Traditional retail systems may have fragmented workflows across billing software, spreadsheets, stock registers, accounting applications and manual barcode generation.

This creates several operational problems:

- Sales and stock quantities become inconsistent.
- Cashiers need to search manually for products.
- Purchasing decisions depend on incomplete stock information.
- Expiring, damaged and slow-moving inventory can be overlooked.
- Supplier dues, expenses and cash movements are difficult to reconcile.
- The owner cannot conveniently monitor the store remotely.
- Internet or network failures can interrupt billing.
- Creating and printing product labels is time-consuming.
- Reports may not reflect actual transactions or accounting balances.

The proposed system will bring these workflows into one coordinated platform.

## 1.3 Product objectives

1. Provide fast, reliable billing across 2–3 Windows counters.
2. Maintain centralized and auditable inventory records.
3. Automate purchasing and supplier-payment workflows.
4. Support Indian retail tax and accounting requirements.
5. Provide barcode-based product identification and label printing.
6. Allow billing to continue during defined network outages.
7. Introduce safe, practical offline voice commands.
8. Give the owner access to sales, stock, cash and business reports remotely.
9. Maintain recoverable backups and a clear transaction history.
10. Establish an architecture that can be expanded without replacing the core system.

## 1.4 Success criteria

The system will be considered ready for initial production when:

- All three intended counters can operate against the central server.
- Sales update stock and accounting correctly.
- Duplicate synchronization does not create duplicate finalized sales.
- Offline transactions are preserved through application and PC restarts.
- GST calculations pass approved test cases.
- Receipts and labels print reliably on the selected hardware.
- Role permissions prevent unauthorized financial or administrative operations.
- Backups can be restored and verified.
- The owner can inspect synchronized business data remotely.
- The store's representative end-to-end acceptance tests pass.

## 2.1 Included in the product

The initial product includes:

- Product, category, brand and department management
- Barcode and SKU management
- Customer and supplier records
- Purchase orders and goods receipts
- Supplier invoices and purchase returns
- Inventory, batches and expiry tracking
- Stock adjustments and physical stock counts
- Windows desktop POS
- Multi-counter operations
- Cash, UPI, card and split payments
- Sales returns, refunds and exchanges
- Cashier shifts and cash reconciliation
- Loyalty, memberships and store credit
- Expenses and cashbook workflows
- Accounting integration and financial reporting

## 2.2 Excluded from the initial release

The following are not launch requirements unless separately approved:

- Franchise management

- Guaranteed stock reservation across disconnected counters
- Fully automated financial decisions made by voice

These may be considered in later releases.

# 4. System Architecture

## 4.1 Architecture overview

The system will use a central ERP backend with custom clients and supporting services.

```text
                    STORE OWNER
                         |
                 Mobile App / Web
                         |
                 Secure Remote API
                         |
                  Cloud Access Layer
                         |
                  Store ERP API
                         |
       +-----------------+-----------------+
       |                 |                 |
   POS Counter 1     POS Counter 2     POS Counter 3
   Windows App      Windows App       Windows App
       |                 |                 |
    SQLite DB         SQLite DB         SQLite DB
    Local Outbox      Local Outbox      Local Outbox
       |                 |                 |
       +-----------------+-----------------+
                         |
                  Store LAN / Ethernet
                         |
                Main Store Server
                         |
                 ERPNext / Frappe
                         |
                      MariaDB
                         |
              GST and Compliance App
```

The diagram is conceptual. Cloud access, synchronization and service placement will be finalized during technical design.

## 4.2 Backend foundation

**Selected foundation:** ERPNext and Frappe.

ERPNext will be responsible for established business operations such as:

- Product and party masters
- Purchasing and stock transactions
- Sales transactions
- Stock valuation
- Payment and accounting workflows
- Financial reports
- Standard document lifecycle and permissions

A custom Frappe application will hold store-specific extensions, server-side operations, integrations and additional reports.

The India Compliance application will be evaluated for the selected ERPNext release and applicable Indian GST requirements.

### Backend rules

- Do not modify ERPNext core files unnecessarily.
- Do not write directly to ERPNext's database tables from the POS.
- Use supported APIs or custom server-side business methods.
- Use the ERP's transaction lifecycle for posting stock and accounting effects.
- Keep custom code in version-controlled applications.
- Validate app compatibility before upgrading ERPNext.

## 4.3 Windows POS client

**Proposed stack:**

- Tauri
- React
- TypeScript
- SQLite
- Rust where native functionality is required

Electron remains an acceptable alternative if its device integrations or development requirements prove more suitable.

The POS must provide:

- Fast product lookup
- Barcode scanner support
- Cart and payment screens
- Receipt printing
- Local transaction persistence
- Offline status display
- Synchronization status
- Voice command interface
- Label printing controls

## 4.4 Main store server

The preferred production arrangement is a dedicated Linux host or supported Linux environment running ERPNext.

The server should provide:

- Central ERP API
- MariaDB database
- Background workers
- Local network access
- Backup scheduling
- Synchronization endpoints
- Printer or device services when required

A Windows PC can be used for development, but the final server arrangement must be validated against the official deployment requirements.

## 4.5 Cloud and mobile layer

The owner dashboard may use a custom web interface, a mobile-friendly Frappe interface, or a dedicated mobile application.

A native mobile application is a later-phase feature, not a prerequisite for getting billing operational.

The cloud layer must not become a mandatory dependency for local billing.

# 5. Functional Requirements

Priority definitions:

- **P0:** Required for the initial operational release.
- **P1:** Required for a complete departmental-store system.
- **P2:** Enhancement after the core system is stable.

## 5.1 Product and department management

**Priority: P0/P1**

### Requirements

- Create, edit, deactivate and search products.
- Assign products to departments and categories.
- Maintain SKU, barcode, name, brand, size, weight and unit.
- Support piece, packet, bottle, kilogram, gram, litre and other configured units.
- Configure purchase units and sales units where applicable.
- Maintain MRP, selling price, purchase price and permitted price lists.
- Store HSN/SAC and applicable tax configuration.
- Support manufacturer barcodes and store-generated internal barcodes.
- Maintain product images where useful.
- Import products from validated CSV or spreadsheet templates.
- Record price changes with user, time and reason.
- Restrict edits to authorized roles.

### Acceptance criteria

- Every sellable product has a unique internal item identifier.
- Barcode lookup resolves to the correct product.
- Duplicate internal identifiers are rejected.
- Inactive products cannot be added to new sales unless explicitly permitted by a documented rule.
- Price changes do not silently alter historical invoices.

## 5.2 Customer management

The system must support:

- Customer purchase history
- Loyalty membership

## 5.3 Supplier management

## 5.5 Inventory management

**Priority: P0/P1**

### Requirements

- Central stock ledger
- Warehouse and location configuration
- Stock on hand
- Batch and expiry tracking
- Damaged and expired stock handling
- Stock adjustment requests
- Physical stock counts
- Stock discrepancy reports
- Stock movement history
- Reserved stock where supported
- Low-stock alerts
- Reorder recommendations
- Stock valuation reports
- Slow-moving inventory reports
- Product-wise movement history

### Stock calculation

The reporting layer should distinguish:

- Physical quantity on hand
- Reserved quantity
- Quantity available for sale
- Outstanding purchase quantity
- Stock in transit, where applicable

These values must use consistent ERP definitions.

### Reorder recommendations

Initial reorder-point logic:

`Reorder point = Average daily demand × Supplier lead time + Safety stock`

The system should also consider:

- Current stock
- Outstanding purchase orders
- Recent sales
- Supplier minimum order quantity
- Purchase pack sizes
- Expiry risk
- Seasonality, if enough reliable data exists

Recommendations must not automatically commit the store to purchasing. An authorized user approves the order.

### Acceptance criteria

Every posted stock movement must be traceable to its source transaction. Stock adjustments require a reason and an authorized user.

## 5.6 Batch and expiry management

**Priority: P1**

Requirements:

- Capture batch number and expiry date for applicable products.
- Support multiple batches of one product.
- Report items nearing expiry.
- Report expired inventory.
- Support configurable alert periods.
- Identify stock by batch during returns and stock adjustments.
- Warn or block the sale of expired products according to configured policy.
- Support batch-aware stock valuation where applicable.

The expiry alert schedule must be configurable, for example 30, 15 and 7 days before expiry.

Products that do not use batch or expiry tracking must not be forced into unnecessary batch workflows.

### POS screen layout

- Search and barcode input
- Current cart
- Quantity and price controls
- Discount information
- Tax and total summary
- Payment action
- Receipt status
- Counter and cashier identity
- Offline or online indicator

### Billing performance targets

These are proposed engineering targets and must be validated on the chosen hardware:

## 5.15 Voice assistant

**Priority: P2 after billing reliability is established**

### Proposed stack

- Offline speech recognition using `whisper.cpp`, `faster-whisper` or Vosk.
- Product matching using the local catalog.
- Rule-based intent extraction.
- RapidFuzz or equivalent matching for spoken product names.
- Explicit validation before execution.

### Supported commands

Examples:

- "Find Colgate toothpaste."
- "Add two Colgate toothpaste, 200 grams."
- "How many packets of rice are available?"
- "Print 20 labels for this product."
- "Show today's sales."

### Command-processing workflow

1. Capture audio.
2. Transcribe speech locally.
3. Extract intent, product, quantity and parameters.
4. Match the product against the local catalog.
5. Check ambiguity and permissions.
6. Display or speak the interpreted command.
7. Ask for confirmation where needed.
8. Execute through the approved application operation.
9. Report success or failure.

### Safety rules

- Do not silently choose between similarly named products.
- Do not allow voice commands to bypass role permissions.
- Require confirmation for destructive, financial or bulk actions.
- Never treat speech transcription as proof that a transaction succeeded.
- Keep the interface usable without voice.

Voice recognition quality must be evaluated using actual store noise, accents, product names and representative commands.

# 6. Offline Operation and Synchronization

Offline support is one of the most important and technically challenging requirements.

## 6.1 Offline capability

Each Windows counter will have its own SQLite database containing:

- Cached product catalog
- Barcodes and search aliases
- Approved price data
- Applicable cached tax configuration
- Counter configuration
- Local draft bills
- Offline transaction queue
- Synchronization status
- Local operational logs

The local database must use appropriate transactional writes and integrity checks.

## 6.2 Source of truth

The central ERP server is the authoritative source for finalized inventory and accounting records.

A counter's local database is authoritative only for its locally recorded pending operations until the server accepts them.

The cloud dashboard is a remote reporting and access layer. It must not independently create a second authoritative inventory ledger.

## 6.3 Transaction states

Every sale must have an explicit state such as:

- `DRAFT`
- `LOCAL_SAVED`
- `PENDING_SYNC`
- `SYNCING`
- `SERVER_COMMITTED`
- `SYNC_FAILED`
- `REQUIRES_REVIEW`
- `CANCELLED`

A locally saved bill must not be presented as a centrally committed transaction.

## 6.4 Outbox and idempotency

Every local transaction must receive a unique transaction identifier, preferably a UUID.

The synchronization process must:

1. Save the transaction durably in SQLite.
2. Add a corresponding outbox event in the same local database transaction.
3. Send the event to the central server.
4. Validate it on the server.
5. Process it through the approved ERP business workflow.
6. Persist the server-side result and idempotency record atomically.
7. Return the result to the counter.
8. Mark the local transaction as committed only after confirmation.

If a response is lost after the server commits, retrying the same event must return the existing result rather than create another sale.

A unique client transaction ID alone is not enough: the server must enforce uniqueness and handle retries safely.

## 6.5 Offline stock conflicts

Independent counters cannot guarantee a globally accurate stock quantity while disconnected.

The system must support an explicit policy:

- **Online:** validate current stock against the central ERP.
- **Brief LAN outage:** permit offline sales according to configurable limits and cached stock information.
- **Long outage:** allow the owner to disable offline sales or restrict selected products.
- **Reconnect:** identify overselling or stock conflicts and require reconciliation when necessary.

The system must not silently discard a bill or silently rewrite a completed transaction to conceal an oversell.

## 6.6 Invoice numbering

Invoice numbering must follow the configured ERP invoice-series and applicable legal requirements.

Offline transactions may need provisional local identifiers. The central server must assign or validate the final official invoice identity through an approved workflow.

The implementation must prevent duplicate finalized invoice numbers across counters. The exact strategy must be established before enabling offline production billing.

## 6.7 Synchronization monitoring

The application must show:

- Current connection state
- Number of pending transactions
- Oldest pending transaction age
- Last successful synchronization
- Failed transactions
- Transactions requiring review

Administrators must have a way to inspect and safely retry failed operations.

# 7. Security, Privacy and Reliability

## 7.1 Security requirements

- Authenticate all server requests.
- Enforce permissions on the backend, not only in the UI.
- Store credentials and API tokens securely.
- Use TLS for remote connections.
- Restrict ERP services to trusted network interfaces where appropriate.
- Use a secure remote access method rather than exposing MariaDB directly to the internet.
- Apply least-privilege access to services and users.
- Maintain audit logs for sensitive operations.
- Encrypt backups and protect their credentials.
- Keep software dependencies and operating systems updated.

## 7.2 Backup requirements

The backup strategy must include:

- Scheduled database backups
- Required files and attachments
- Encrypted offsite copies
- Backup success/failure notifications
- Retention policies
- Periodic restore testing
- Documented recovery procedures

A backup that has never been restored and verified is not sufficient evidence of recoverability.

## 7.3 Proposed service objectives

These are initial targets to validate, not guarantees.

| Metric | Proposed target |
|---|---|
| Local cached product lookup | Under 200 ms at p95 |
| Online local-network sale submission | Under 2 seconds at p95 under normal load, excluding external dependencies |
| Offline transaction durability | Survives application restart and ordinary PC restart after successful local commit |
| Duplicate sale creation on retry | Zero |
| Core local billing availability | Must not depend on cloud availability |
| Recovery point objective | Target maximum 15 minutes of central data loss, subject to backup design |
| Recovery time objective | Target service restoration within 4 hours |
| Backup restoration | Verified in scheduled recovery drills |

Recovery objectives must be approved against the store's budget, equipment and operational tolerance.

## 7.4 Auditability

The system must record:

- User
- Timestamp
- Counter or device
- Action performed
- Source transaction
- Before/after values where appropriate
- Approval details
- Failure and retry information

Sensitive logs must avoid unnecessary exposure of customer information and credentials.

# 10. Data Model

The initial logical data model includes the following entities. ERPNext's existing DocTypes should be reused wherever they meet the requirements.

| Entity | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Company | Legal and accounting entity |
| Department | Store-specific department |
| Item | Product master |
| Barcode | Product identifiers |
| Item Group | Product category hierarchy |
| Warehouse | Inventory location |
| Batch | Batch and expiry information |
| Customer | Customer record |
| Supplier | Supplier record |
| Price List | Selling or buying prices |
| Purchase Order | Supplier order |
| Purchase Receipt | Goods received |
| Purchase Invoice | Supplier invoice |
| Sales Invoice / POS transaction | Sale and financial transaction |
| Payment Entry | Payment recording |
| Return / Credit Note | Reversal or return transaction |
| Stock Ledger Entry | Stock movement history |
| Loyalty Transaction | Points earned or redeemed |
| Cashier Shift | Shift and cash reconciliation |
| Expense | Expense transaction |
| User / Role | Access control |
| Audit Event | Traceable system actions |
| Counter Device | Registered POS installation |
| Offline Outbox Event | Pending synchronization event |
| Sync Result | Server acceptance or failure result |
| Label Job | Requested label print job |
| Voice Command Log | Minimal operational command record |

### Data-model principles

- Reuse ERPNext document types instead of duplicating core ledgers.
- Keep locally queued transactions distinguishable from posted ERP documents.
- Preserve transaction lineage for returns and reversals.
- Use stable identifiers across counters.
- Enforce uniqueness in the authoritative database.
- Version custom schema changes through migrations.
- Avoid storing sensitive payment credentials in the POS.

# 15. Risks and Mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| ERPNext workflow differs from POS expectations | Build an early proof of concept against the exact deployed version |
| Custom POS submits incomplete accounting data | Use supported ERP business operations and integration tests |
| Offline sales create stock conflicts | Explicit offline policies, conflict detection and reconciliation |
| Duplicate invoices after retries | Server-enforced idempotency and transactional processing |
| Invoice numbers collide across counters | Finalize a compliant numbering strategy before production |
| GST workflow is incomplete | Validate applicable rules and compliance integration with an accountant |
| Printer compatibility problems | Test real devices before finalizing the printing stack |
| Voice misidentifies a product | Match confidence thresholds and confirmation for ambiguous commands |
| Cloud outage blocks billing | Keep central local-server billing independent of cloud access |
| Server disk or power failure | UPS, backups, monitoring and restoration drills |
| ERPNext upgrade breaks customizations | Version pinning, compatibility tests and staged upgrades |
| Reporting numbers are misleading | Document definitions and reconcile against ERP records |
| Poor network quality | Wired LAN, network diagnostics and durable local queues |

# 16. Deployment and Operations

## 16.1 Development

- Use a dedicated development environment.
- Never test with live financial data unless explicitly authorized and appropriately protected.
- Keep secrets outside source control.
- Use synthetic test products and transactions.
- Version database migrations and configuration.

## 16.2 Pilot

- Deploy to one counter first.
- Verify product catalog and tax configuration.
- Compare sample bills against the approved workflow.
- Test receipt printing.
- Simulate LAN outages.
- Reconcile all test transactions.
- Expand to the other counters after approval.

## 16.3 Production

- Configure automated service startup.
- Configure backup and monitoring alerts.
- Restrict server and database access.
- Keep the POS and backend versions compatible.
- Maintain a rollback procedure.
- Train staff in normal billing, returns and offline recovery.
- Document emergency procedures for prolonged server outages.

# 20. Open Decisions Before Production

The architecture is defined at a high level, but the following details need confirmation during implementation:

1. Exact product count and expected number of bills per day.
2. Number of departments and whether multiple storage locations are needed.
3. Selected receipt and label printer models.
4. Whether the main server will be a dedicated Linux machine or a supported Linux environment on existing hardware.
5. Whether offline billing may continue indefinitely or should be restricted after a configurable duration.
6. Exact tax and invoice requirements for the store's business registration.
7. Whether customer credit and loyalty are required at launch.
8. Preferred mobile approach: PWA or native mobile application.
9. Backup retention, cloud provider and recovery objectives.
10. Whether future branches are a real requirement or simply an architectural possibility.

These decisions should be recorded before the relevant feature is released.

# 21. Final Product Definition

The intended deliverable is a **custom departmental store business management platform built around ERPNext**, with a purpose-built Windows POS, independent local SQLite queues, controlled offline synchronization, barcode and printer integration, Indian GST workflows, inventory and purchasing management, financial reporting, voice-assisted operations and secure owner access.

The implementation order is deliberate: establish the ERP foundation, prove one complete sale, make it reliable offline, expand to multiple counters, and then add the broader business-management and automation features.

**The first engineering milestone is not the dashboard or voice assistant. It is one correctly processed sale that remains safe under failure conditions.** That transaction becomes the foundation for the rest of the system.
